A chapter page in one block: the series and chapter, the pages as a two-page spread, how far the reader is, and a drawer with every chapter of the series. Put it on each chapter's page, with [Volume Grid](/docs/blocks/volume-grid) and [Chapter List](/docs/blocks/chapter-list) on the series page.

It is the first block made of blocks: it composes [Spread Viewer](/docs/blocks/spread-viewer) and [Chapter List](/docs/blocks/chapter-list), declared by URL like any item, with drawer, progress, button and kbd, and draws nothing of its own. The header holds the series and the chapter's number and title, with a Chapters button that opens a drawer from the bottom edge; the drawer lists the series' chapters with chapter-list and says where you are. Under the spread a progress bar fills up through the chapter. `direction` and `single` go to the spread viewer as they are.

The reader is the page's one `main` region by default, named by its heading; inside a page that already has a main (as these demos do, on a docs page), pass `as="section"` and it becomes a region with an `h2`. Only the spread takes the arrow keys, and only while it has focus: the header, the drawer and the rest of the page keep theirs. The progress bar's value text is the same words it shows, "Page 5 of 24", counting the last page on screen. The drawer is drawer's dialog: it traps focus while open, closes on Escape, and returns focus to the Chapters button.

## The page in the address

`useReader`, from `use-reader.ts` (also re-exported by the entry file), holds the reader's page. Give it `url` with a `read` and a `write` and it keeps the page in `?page=` (from 1), reading it once after the page has hydrated and writing every turn, other parameters kept. The block never touches `window` or a router itself: you wire them.

```tsx
"use client";

const reader = useReader({
  count: pages.length,
  url: {
    read: () => window.location.search,
    // Next.js picks replaceState up: usePathname and useSearchParams follow.
    write: (search) => window.history.replaceState(window.history.state, "", search),
  },
});

<Reader {...chapterProps} page={reader.page} onPageChange={reader.setPage} />;
```

Without `url` the hook keeps the page in state; without `page` the reader keeps its own. `readPageParam`, `writePageParam` and `readerProgress` are exported for a router of your own.
